Hand the boring work to an agent

Weekly report, new contact, invoice, new client: tasks that take hours and don't call for genius. In eight short lessons, you'll find your first task to delegate, write the agent sheet, decide which model does what, and check the result before anything goes out.

The lessons

From a list of boring tasks to a small team of agents in 30 days. Each lesson has a diagram, a template to copy, practice with a "done when" checklist, and a walkthrough of the test.

  1. Lesson 1 · 15 minFind the right boring taskYou don’t need to automate your whole business. You need one task: the one that takes time every week, feels tedious, and won’t cause damage if it goes wrong. In this lesson, AI interviews you about your week and makes a list; you choose the first task.
  2. Lesson 2 · 15 minThe agent sheet on one pageAn agent without instructions does what it thinks you want. The agent sheet is the page that says who it is, when to work, what steps to follow, what never to do, and how to deliver the work. In this course, that’s what an agent is: a chat Project (or a folder in the terminal) with the sheet in its instructions.
  3. Lesson 3 · 18 minThe weekly report without opening the dashboardEvery Monday, someone checks the dashboard, copies numbers, and writes a summary. It’s ideal boring work: read-only, every week, and checked in two minutes. Have a website? The dashboard is Search Console, Google’s free tool that shows how people find your website. Don’t have one? Use the sheet for this week’s numbers. You export the data, and the agent reads and writes.
  4. Lesson 4 · 16 minNew contact becomes a draft, never a sent messageA contact who reaches out on Friday and only gets a reply on Tuesday may lose interest. Reading each message, noting it in the spreadsheet, and writing a reply is boring work, but the reply speaks to the customer: that’s why the agent prepares everything as a draft and stops. You read it and press send.
  5. Lesson 5 · 16 minInvoice and bill: you handle the moneyOpening each vendor invoice, copying the number, due date, and amount into the accounts payable spreadsheet is one of the most tedious tasks of the week. The agent can read and add them up, flag duplicate invoices, and find totals that don't match. What it never does is pay: the list comes out as a draft, and the bank is yours.
  6. Lesson 6 · 15 minNew client: the checklist that stops when something failsYou signed a new client. Now you need to update the contact record, create a folder, set up the schedule, prepare the first invoice, and write a welcome message. It's the same five steps every time, and forgetting one can be costly. The agent goes through the checklist in order, doesn't create anything twice, and stops at the step that fails.
  7. Lesson 7 · 15 minLight model for routine work, top model for the hard stuffYou don't call your most experienced business partner to copy a spreadsheet. Models work the same way: the light model is fast and uses less of your plan, and it handles routine work; the top model thinks more slowly and is for the big plan, the hard problem, and anything that could cost a lot. The routing rule spells this out once.
  8. Lesson 8 · 14 minFrom your first agent to a small team in 30 daysThe most common mistake is setting up five agents on the first weekend and stopping the review by Tuesday. The approach that works starts slowly: one task, review everything, build trust, and only then add the next. In four weeks, you'll have a small team working while you review a sample of its work.

Who it's for

  1. 1Small business owners or freelancers who lose hours to reports, spreadsheets, and repetitive emails.
  2. 2People who already use ChatGPT or Claude in chat and want to move from one-off questions to complete tasks.
  3. 3People who don't code: every practice exercise has a chat-based path. The terminal (Claude Code or Codex) is optional.

What you'll be able to do

  1. 1Choose your first task to delegate based on time, boredom, and risk.
  2. 2Write the agent sheet on one page, with safety rules.
  3. 3Create the weekly report, contact list, and bills to pay as drafts.
  4. 4Decide which model handles routine work and which one you save for difficult tasks.
  5. 5Follow a 30-day plan, checking everything at first and then sampling the results.

Where to do it

  1. 1In chat: a ChatGPT or Claude Project (or a Gemini Gem) with the agent sheet in its instructions. In this course, that’s what we call an agent: it always does the same task, the same way. Projects and Gems may depend on your plan; without them, paste the sheet as the first message in each new conversation.
  2. 2In the terminal (optional): Claude Code or Codex, in a folder with the files you exported.
  3. 3All through the subscription you already have. No API key and nothing extra to pay.
