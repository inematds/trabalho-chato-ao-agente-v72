Lesson 5 · 16 min

Invoices and bills: the money passes through you

Opening each supplier invoice, copying the number, due date, and amount into the accounts payable spreadsheet is one of the most boring tasks of the week. The agent can read and calculate, flag duplicate invoices, and flag totals that don’t match. The one thing it never does is pay: the list comes out as a draft, and the bank is yours to handle.

Start the lesson

In this lesson, you’ll

  1. 1Pull the details from each invoice or bill
  2. 2Check whether the line items add up to the total
  3. 3Pick up the invoice that arrived twice
  4. 4Always leave payment to you

Goal: have the agent read invoices and bills and make the accounts payable list, flagging duplicate invoices and totals that don’t match.

02 · The sheet and four sample invoices

The invoices are made up. Two have a problem on purpose.

Sheet · invoices and bills agent
Role: You are my accounts payable agent.
About me: [what the business does]; frequent suppliers and categories: [Lumberyard = raw materials; Accounting = services].
When: when I attach or paste the week’s invoices and bills and say "invoices".
Steps: 1) for each document, pull out the supplier, number, date, due date, items, and total; 2) add up the items and compare them with the printed total; 3) check whether the same supplier with the same number has already appeared in this batch or in the list of previously entered invoices I paste; 4) make a table: supplier, number, due date, total, category, status; 5) add up the week’s total using only invoices without problems.
Rules: only read, never change or delete anything; login screen = stop and call me; copy numbers exactly; if the sum doesn’t match or a number is unreadable, mark it "doesn't match" and leave it out of the total; duplicate invoice: mark it "duplicate" and leave it out of the total; never pay, approve, or schedule a payment; never ask for a bank password; everything is a draft for me to check; if you’re unsure, stop and ask.
Delivery: the table sorted by due date and, below it, the week’s total and the list of items left out, with the reason.
First task: run it now with the invoices I’m going to paste.

Simulation: illustrative example
  • Invoice 4471 (Lumberyard): 1,250.00, due Nov 10, OK.
  • Bill 311 (Accounting): 650.00, due Nov 15, OK.
  • Invoice 5120 (Hardware): doesn't match. The items add up to 800.00, but the total says 900.00. Left out of the total.
  • Document 3: invoice 4471 duplicate. Left out of the total.
  • Week’s total: 1,900.00.

These calculations were done by hand using the example. Take invoice 5120 up with the supplier; the agent doesn’t decide which amount is correct.

In the my-agents/invoices folder, with the sheet in AGENTS.md (or CLAUDE.md), put the week’s PDFs in a week subfolder and run codex exec --skip-git-repo-check -m gpt-6-luna -s read-only -o bills.md "invoices: follow AGENTS.md using the files in the week folder".

If a PDF is a scanned image and the light model can’t read it, it should say "couldn’t read" (that’s the rule), and you can send it to the top model or type it in.

  • Letting the agent "fix" the total: it can't know whether the line item or the total is wrong. You resolve it with the vendor.
  • Sending only the total, without the line items: without the line items, it has nothing to add up.
  • Giving it bank access "just to read the statement": that's outside this course's scope. Export the statement if you need to.

06 · The lesson in three sentences

Read and add upThe agent extracts the details from each invoice and checks whether the items add up to the total.
Flag it, don't fix itDuplicates and items marked "doesn't match" stay out of the total, with the reason noted. You handle the issue.
Your bank is yoursThe list comes out as a draft. You handle paying, approving, and scheduling.
