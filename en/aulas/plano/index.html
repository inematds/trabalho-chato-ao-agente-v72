Lesson 8 · 14 min

From your first agent to a small team in 30 days

The most common mistake is setting up five agents on the first weekend and stopping the review by Tuesday. The approach that works starts slowly: one task, review everything, build trust, and only then add the next. In four weeks, you'll have a small team working while you review a sample of its work.

02 · Check everything, then sample

The proof it worked is the log, not your impression. Click each phase.

1. Everything

For each agent’s first three rounds, check the entire result: every number, every draft. For a weekly task, that means three weeks; for a daily task, three days.

Every correction you make becomes a rule on the sheet.

2. Sample

Three consecutive runs with no corrections? Switch to sample checks: two numbers from the report, one out of every three drafts, two notes from the table.

The gate never goes away: you keep reading anything that talks to a client or handles money.

3. Back

Found an error in the sample? That task goes back to "check everything" until three consecutive runs match again.

The error becomes a rule on the sheet before the next run.

4. Log

One line per run: date, agent, what you checked, whether it matched, and what changed on the sheet.

In a month, the log shows how much time you saved and where the agent makes mistakes.

Log entry11/11 | report | checked 2 numbers | matched | no changes

A note, a spreadsheet, or a diario.txt file. One line per run.

25/11 | website report | light | everything (run 3) | yes | none | 55
02/12 | website report | light | 2 numbers (sample) | yes | none | 55
Friday review (paste into the chat with the check log)
Here’s my check log for the week.
Tell me, in no more than 8 lines:
1. How many minutes the agents saved in total (add up the last column).
2. Which agent needed the most corrections and what rule I should add to their sheet.
3. Which agent is ready to move to spot checks (three rounds in a row with no corrections).
Don’t make up numbers: use only what’s in the log.
Simulation: illustrative example

In chat, you’re in charge of the schedule: the plan below creates an .ics file with reminders for your calendar ("Monday 9 a.m.: report"). Getting the agent to run on its own at the scheduled time takes a terminal step, which the course From chat to agent shows you. Start with the reminder: calling the agent at the right time already takes the boring work off your hands.

  • Five agents in the first week: you can’t check everything, and the first mistake slips through.
  • Stop checking because "it’s always worked": spot checks mean checking less, not stopping.
  • No log: after a month, you won’t know whether the agent saves time or just shifts the work around.
